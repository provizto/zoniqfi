Investor Due Diligence: Hard Questions & Model Defense Executive FAQ

Q1: Why does Phase 3 represent over 50% of the aggregate raise?
Defense: Phase 3 funds are predominantly Capital Expenditure (CapEx) rather than operational burn. Up to 75% of Phase 3 is deployed directly into Protocol-Owned Liquidity (paired in USDC and SOL). This capital never leaves the protocol balance sheet; it stabilizes the token's market floor, generates recurring LP swap revenue for the treasury, and provides verifiable asset backing for early backers.
Q2: Why raise a lean $190k–$290k instead of a typical $1.5M+ pre-seed round?
Defense: All 4 modular contracts (Swap, Lock, Vault, Affiliate) are already functional on Solana Devnet. Because our core R&D phase is largely complete, we do not require excessive runway. Raising lean capital tied strictly to verifiable milestones eliminates premature equity/token dilution, guaranteeing maximum valuation upside for early investors when scaling into Mainnet metrics.
Q3: How is key mismanagement or "rug-pull" risk mitigated post-funding?
Defense: In Phase 2, all single-key deployer authority is permanently revoked. Program Upgrade Authority and treasury PDAs are transferred to a 3-of-5 Squads v4 Multisig composed of core developers, investor representatives (Oryon), and independent security advisors. No transaction or upgrade can execute unilaterally.
Q4: How does the protocol sustain runway after Phase 4 without continuous fundraising?
Defense: ZoniqFi features a self-sustaining cash flow flywheel. Every DEX trade incurs a 0.3% flat fee, with 15% automatically routed to the Operational Treasury PDA in real-time. At a baseline daily volume of $2M–$3M, the protocol generates sufficient monthly operating revenue in USDC to cover high-speed RPCs and engineering overhead without minting inflationary tokens.
